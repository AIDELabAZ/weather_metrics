\begin{figure}[!htbp]
	\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
		\caption{Household Fixed Effects and the Incremental Search Gap}
		\label{app:front_increment}
		{\centering
			\includegraphics[width=.97\linewidth,keepaspectratio]{Figures/front_increment_forest.pdf}\par}
		\smallskip
		\footnotesize \textit{Note}: Define
		$G_s(K)=|r_{\mathrm{train},s}(K)|-\mathrm{sign}(r_{\mathrm{train},s}(K))
		r_{\mathrm{heldout},s}(K)$ and $\Delta G_s=G_s(84)-G_s(1)$.
		The plotted statistic is $D=\Delta G_{\mathrm{year+HH\ FE}}-
		\Delta G_{\mathrm{year\ FE}}$, paired within the same allocation, held-out
		fold and candidate ordering before aggregation. Positive values mean
		household FE increases the search-induced training--validation gap;
		negative values mean it reduces that gap. Points are medians and whiskers
		are descriptive 10th--90th percentiles across 100 geographic fold/allocation
		summaries, after within-fold medians over 20 orderings. All 100 summaries
		are valid; Niger retains 1,993 value and 1,992 maize raw pairs, versus
		2,000 elsewhere. These are neither confidence intervals nor coefficient
		or IV estimates. Repeated folds reuse observations.
	\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

			\footnotesize \textit{Note}: Panel A shows mean standardized first-stage

			for each rainfall metric. Distance bins are equally spaced categories;
			vertical scales differ across countries. Panel B separates outcomes,
			using year and household FE and 84 matched specifications per
			country/outcome. Each covariance comparison retains the same coefficient
			and observations. The 100-km Bartlett adjustment adds contemporaneous
			cross-household spatial covariance to unrestricted own-household serial
			covariance. Threshold shares are descriptive, not tests of differences.
			Geographic restrictions remain substantial in Tanzania and Uganda;
			missing or confidential coordinates are not imputed.
